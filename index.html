What is an Election?

Understanding democracy in the simplest words

⚖️

One Person, One Vote

Every adult citizen has exactly one vote, and every single vote has equal value. Nobody's vote is more powerful than yours.

🔒

Secret Ballot

Your vote is 100% confidential. No one—not your boss, family, or police—can ever find out which candidate you chose.

🕊️

Peaceful Change

Elections allow citizens to renew their leaders or choose new ones every 5 years without conflict.

💡 Think of it like choosing a team captain!

Everyone gets together, looks at the options, and casts a secret token. The person with the most trust and votes represents the entire group.

2

Who Can Vote?

Eligibility criteria to be an Indian voter

🇮🇳

Indian Citizen

You must be a legal citizen of India. Both resident and registered overseas citizens are included.

🎂

18+ Years Old

You must turn 18 on or before the qualifying dates (January 1, April 1, July 1, or October 1).

📜

Registered in Voter List

Crucial Rule: Having a Voter ID card is not enough! Your name must be enrolled in the Electoral Roll of your constituency.

🧠

Sound Mind

Must not be disqualified by law due to mental unsoundness or conviction for electoral offences.

Before Voting: How to Prepare

3 crucial checks to complete before polling day

🔍

1. Search Your Name

Check on the Voter Helpline App or voters.eci.gov.in. Download your Voter Information Slip containing your serial and part number.

📍

2. Locate Your Polling Booth

Your polling booth is usually a nearby school or community center. Know its exact location so you don't wander on voting day.

🪪

3. Carry an Approved Photo ID

Voter ID (EPIC) is ideal, but you can also use Aadhaar, Driving License, PAN Card, Passport, or MNREGA Job Card.

💳 Valid Photo IDs Accepted at Polling Station:

🪪 Voter ID (EPIC)
🆔 Aadhaar Card
🚗 Driving License
🛂 Passport
💳 PAN Card
🏦 Bank Passbook with Photo

Voting Day: Step-by-Step at the Booth

What will happen when you enter the Polling Station

1
Station 1

First Polling Officer

Checks your Photo ID and verifies your name against the official electoral roll. The officer calls out your name loudly.

2
Station 2

Second Polling Officer (Inking & Signature)

Applies indelible purple ink on your left index finger. You sign or provide your thumb impression in Register 17A.

🟣 Indelible Purple Ink Mark on Left Index Finger
3
Station 3

Third Polling Officer (Voter Slip)

Collects the voter slip, checks the ink mark on your finger, and guides you to the private voting compartment.

4
Station 4

Secret Voting Compartment

You step inside the three-sided shielded cubicle. Complete privacy is guaranteed by law. No cameras or phones are permitted.

5
Station 5

Cast Vote on EVM & Check VVPAT

Press the blue button next to your chosen candidate. A red light turns on, followed by a long BEEP. The VVPAT window shows your printed slip for 7 seconds!

The Election Timeline

Major milestones from announcement to results

STAGE 1

1. Announcement

Election Commission announces dates. The Model Code of Conduct takes effect immediately.

STAGE 2

2. Nomination

Candidates file papers. Officials scrutinize eligibility and candidates can withdraw.

STAGE 3

3. Campaigning

Candidates present manifestos. Loudspeaker campaigning stops 48 hours before voting begins.

STAGE 4

4. Polling Day

Citizens cast votes safely from morning to evening using EVMs and VVPATs.

STAGE 5

5. Counting & Results

Votes are counted under strict transparency. Winning representatives are declared.

Common Questions & Myth Busters

Facts you should know before stepping into the booth

YES! If your name is on the Electoral Roll, you can vote by showing any of the 12 approved government photo IDs such as Aadhaar Card, Driving License, Passport, PAN Card, or Bank Passbook.
NO ONE can know who you voted for. The voting compartment is fully enclosed, EVMs do not record voter identity, and ballot secrecy is strictly protected under Section 128 of the Representation of the People Act.
NOTA is the last option on the EVM ballot. It allows a voter to officially record their right not to vote for any of the contesting candidates while still participating in democracy.
Bribery or coercion is a severe criminal offense. Your vote is your sovereign right. Call the 1950 toll-free helpline or report anonymously on the cVIGIL App.

Available in all Indian languages for voter registration, polling booth queries, and reporting complaints.
